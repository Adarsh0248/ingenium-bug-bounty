Authored question categories continue to determine region families across languages; existing capture receipts are retained. The 50-bounty support already being worked on is not removed. No persistent database or cloud resources were changed for this rollback.

## Enlarged region exploration

Each focused area is four viewport widths by four viewport heights at a fixed scale: a 100 × 100 viewing window shows one section of a 400 × 400 world. Drag or scroll to pan; Shift+scroll moves horizontally. Directional buttons and Centre provide equivalent controls. Focus the map and use arrow keys or Home for keyboard navigation. There are no map zoom controls or zoom shortcuts. Each region keeps its camera position when switching away and back. The overview remains the four separate area cards.

The SVG scenery and creature models use the same map transform. Up to 30 targets in one area start spread out across the map, then roam throughout the region. Targets outside the visible portion are excluded from drawing, and a drag is suppressed as a question click. The overview tiles show scenery and counts only; their entire surface opens the full area without capturing a bug. Thirty targets per area describes map space, not a change from the existing 50-bounty event cap.

Focused areas use the shared [steering simulation](bug-movement.md), with independent destinations, acceleration, turning, short rests and size-aware collision avoidance. Position, heading and gait memory survive switching regions and returning from the overview. Hidden regions pause, and the overview has no creature layer. Hover, open-workbench freezes and reduced motion retain their holds. The delayed termination camera uses the stored actual position. This visual simulation does not affect habitat categories, question difficulty, bounty or judging.

The organic regions mix six silhouettes: wasps/moths hover with moving wings; dragonflies dart; beetles walk; mantises hop; weavers skitter; segmented grubs drag and stretch slowly. Individual pace varies within each type, and ground creatures rest their legs when wandering pauses. Foundry instead uses the [armoured mechanical fleet](ember-foundry.md), with bounty-dependent size, speed and cosmetic weapons. Species assignment is shared across languages by bounty identity; organic movement speed remains independent of bounty value. Region assignment still follows the authored category; legacy uncategorised bounties retain their previous region.

Rarity follows the actual frozen base bounty: Common 10-390 CR, Rare 400-690, Elite 700-890 and Apex 900-1000. Higher ranks have stronger silhouettes, explicit labels and progressively stronger auras. Neon uses signal halos, Silicon Hive uses circuit coronas, Verdant Nexus uses branching signals and Zero-Day Core uses intrusion fields. The target list includes the same silhouette/rank plus a compact threshold guide. Auras are cosmetic, and reduced motion freezes their animation.

## Neon runtime world

Neon is a directly drawn 2D circuit world in midnight blue, cyan, magenta and violet. It contains array banks, heap cells, execution traces, call stacks and registers around a processor core. The world uses the full four-by-four viewport area, with modules sized in world pixels rather than enlarging one illustration. Circuit routes carry eighteen moving data packets, the core ring rotates, and a few signal lights pulse. The existing reduced-motion and manual motion controls pause these effects. At most 48 static modules and fourteen routes bound the scenery work; no glow filters, background videos or image downloads are required.
